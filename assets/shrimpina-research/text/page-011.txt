Lady Crab = Ovalipes ocellatus
   ●​ Description
          ○​ AKA leopard crab/Calico Crab
          ○​ Carapace (Shell): They feature a pale yellowish-gray or light purplish carapace
             covered in distinct, striking clusters of purple or reddish-brown leopard-like spots.
             Their shells grow up to about 3 inches long and slightly wider.
          ○​ Spiny Features: The front edge of their rounded shell is armed with five sharp
             marginal teeth on each side of the head.
          ○​ Paddle Feet: As members of the swimming crab family, their hindmost pair of
             legs are uniquely modified into flat, paddle-like swimmerets that allow them to
             swim expertly through the surf or quickly burrow backwards into the substrate.
          ○​ The "Toe-Pincher" Reputation: According to marine accounts, “They bury in
             the sand and wait for prey, or your toes, to swim by.”. They are famously feisty
             and aggressive. When wading through the shallow sandbars or low-tide flats of
             Woodneck, people frequently step near them, prompting a sharp pinch from their
             elongated, powerful claws.
          ○​ Camouflage: They spend most of the daytime buried completely beneath the sand
             in the shallow surf zone. They leave only their stalked eyes exposed to spy on
             passing prey or watch for predators like seagulls.
          ○​ Diet: They are voracious, opportunistic omnivores. They use their sharp claws to
             hunt small fish and crabs, dig up bivalves like razor clams, or scavenge dead
             organic matter.
   ●​ Location
          ○​ Found in marsh as well as the beach. Beach dwellers were much larger than the
             ones in the marsh. They tended to reside above the sand instead of digging and
             hiding more often like the marsh habitants
