Atlantic Mud Fiddler Crab = Minuca pugnax
    ●​ Description
          ○​ AKA Mud Fiddler Crab / Marsh Fiddler Crab
          ○​ Carapace (Shell): a small, roughly square carapace, usually less than about 20
              mm wide in adults. It is generally dark brown or muddy gray, often with lighter
              speckling. One of the best field marks is a cobalt-blue band or patch across the
              front of the carapace just behind the eyes, although the amount of blue varies
              between individuals.
          ○​ Tall Eyestalks: Like other fiddler crabs, its eyes sit at the ends of long stalks that
              can be raised high above the body. This is especially useful in a marsh
              environment because the crab can remain close to its burrow or low to the mud
              while still watching for predators and nearby crabs.
          ○​ Male’s Enlarged Claw: Males have one dramatically enlarged claw and one
              small claw, while females have two small claws of approximately equal size. The
              enlarged claw is used primarily for courtship displays, territorial signaling, and
              fighting with rival males, rather than for feeding.
          ○​ Feeding With One Hand: Because the male’s major claw is poorly suited for
              gathering sediment, males feed almost entirely using the small claw. They
              repeatedly scoop mud toward the mouth, where specialized mouthparts separate
              edible organic material and microorganisms from the sediment.
          ○​ Mud Specialist: This species strongly favors muddy salt-marsh edges, creek
              banks, and areas among marsh vegetation. Mud and sand fiddlers can overlap, but
              they tend to dominate the muddier sections while sand fiddlers become more
              common as the substrate becomes sandier.
          ○​ Burrows: Mud fiddlers excavate narrow burrows in exposed marsh sediment. The
              burrow provides refuge from predators and extreme temperatures and gives the
              crab somewhere to retreat as environmental conditions change with the tide.
          ○​ Courtship Display: Males stand near their burrows and repeatedly wave the
              enlarged claw to attract females. The exaggerated claw therefore acts as a visual
              signal of reproductive fitness, although carrying such a large structure also
              imposes energetic and mobility costs.
    ●​ Location
          ○​ Found on the outer edges of the marsh. Usually in muddy areas lined with tall
              grass.
    ●​ Notes
          ○​ The males have a larger arm for the purpose of attracting a mate. This larger claw
