How Traits Shape Community Roles

01

Predators & Scavengers:

Blue crabs, lady crabs, and green crabs use strong claws and fast movement to hunt, scavenge, and control smaller organisms.

02

Burrowers & Sediment Mixers:

Fiddler crabs dig burrows and feed through sediment, helping aerate and reshape muddy/sandy marsh edges.

03

Camouflaged Survivors:

Spider crabs, lady crabs, and Asian shore crabs rely on camouflage, hiding, or decoration to avoid predators or catch prey.

04

Shelter Users:

Hermit crabs and pea crabs survive by using other organisms’ shells or bodies as protection.

Key Takeaways

  1. 01

    Different strategies, similar goals

    Crabs may burrow, swim, hide, decorate themselves, or borrow shelter, but all of these behaviors help them survive and reproduce

  2. 02

    Form follows habitat

    Different body shapes, claws, camouflage, and swimming structures reflect the environments each crab occupies.

  3. 03

    No single strategy dominates

    The diversity of crabs across Woodneck Beach and Little Sippewissett Marsh shows that many very different adaptations can succeed within the same coastal ecosystem.
